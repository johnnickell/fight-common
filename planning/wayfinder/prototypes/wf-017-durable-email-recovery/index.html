WF-017: how does required security email survive post-commit failure?

Exercise one invitation delivery from atomic command commit through lost dispatch, recovery, bounded retry, expiry, and resend. The database keeps a grant digest and authenticated ciphertext; workers decrypt only in memory, and a queue message remains only a wake-up hint to load the durable record.

Guided walkthroughs

  1. The invitation, grant digest, encrypted delivery, and required audit commit atomically.
  2. The process crashes before its post-commit event reaches the queue.
  3. A recovery scan finds the durable pending record; one worker leases, sends, acknowledges, and destroys ciphertext.
  1. A worker exclusively leases the pending delivery.
  2. The mail adapter fails; the record becomes retryable and remains discoverable.
  3. After the due time, recovery requeues it and a later attempt succeeds.
  1. The delivery remains pending until its purpose-bound grant expires.
  2. Recovery performs no send after expiry.
  3. Terminal cleanup expires the delivery and destroys recoverable ciphertext.
  1. The original activation delivery fails and remains pending.
  2. Resend atomically revokes the predecessor grant and delivery, destroys its ciphertext, and creates unrelated replacements.
  3. A stale predecessor job no-ops; recovery sends only the replacement.

Candidate verdict: persist the delivery obligation atomically with the command. Queue jobs carry only its opaque ID. Recovery scans durable due records, workers claim with a bounded lease, and delivery is at-least-once—not falsely exactly-once. Success, terminal expiry, and revocation destroy the ciphertext.

A provider may accept a message immediately before the worker crashes. Retrying can produce an equivalent duplicate email. The stable delivery ID supports provider idempotency where available, but correctness relies on one purpose-bound credential and atomic grant consumption, not on an unprovable exactly-once send.

Decision language

                

                    
delivery obligation
A durable record required for eventual security-email execution.

                    
wake-up hint
A queue job containing only the delivery ID; losing it does not lose the obligation.

                    
lease
A compare-and-set claim that prevents concurrent workers from sending in parallel.

                    
at-least-once
A crash after provider acceptance can cause an equivalent retry; the credential remains single-use.
