# Remove retained notices without concealing history loss

## Outcome

Authorized consumer operations can remove explicit bounded selections of retained notices without corrupting
surviving records or concealing the resulting limits of recovery. Deliver removal Commands, independent current
admission, atomic storage/history-loss updates and honest retry/concurrency semantics in one normal PR.

John approved this second vertical slice completing [TICKET-00045](../tickets/00045-TICKET.md)'s decomposition.
Its only blocker is [TASK-00158](00158-TASK.md), supplying dedicated DBAL notice storage and recording/retry/order
contracts. Public polling/history Queries and MCP Tools remain with TICKET-00046, not a reverse dependency.

## Scope

- In scope: bounded consumer-selected removal Commands/handlers, independent current authorization, atomic notice/
  relationship changes and recovery-limit information on SQLite, MySQL and PostgreSQL.
- In scope: repeat/missing-record behavior, removed-identity retry lifecycle, concurrent recording/removal evidence,
  safe post-commit facts where defined, schema/composition, compatibility, docs and complete local verification.
- Out of scope: retention duration/eligibility/holds, scheduling, cleanup-job recovery, archival, dashboards, live MCP
  or Mercure delivery and consumer deployment qualification. A supplied cutoff does not decide Common-owned policy.
- Out of scope: protected-body storage, full Task-state retention, receipt/processing ledgers, indefinite deduplication,
  public mutation Tools or a guarantee that all originating application changes ever produced a notice.

### Accepted storage and recovery mechanics

Extend TASK-00158's dedicated DBAL storage without adding Event Sourcing, projections or a consumer execution ledger.
Removal is over an explicit finite consumer-selected set, not an unbounded sweep. Define finite selection bounds,
identity validation and same-operation all-or-nothing behavior; reject unauthorized selections without partial
mutation or disclosure. Consumers decide which records qualify and when to invoke removal. Common does not infer
eligibility from a date, Workflow outcome, subscriber disconnect or claimed cleanup-caller identity.

Update bounded history-loss information atomically with deletion. It must survive removal of the final notice and
permit TICKET-00046 to distinguish established retained recovery from incomplete/uncertain history. Do not infer
complete catch-up from an empty result or absence of a row. Preserve surviving notices and TASK-00158's dedicated
commit-ordered positions; deleting data must not reset/reuse ordering in a way that makes older continuation appear
complete. Exact recovery metadata/schema and its ordering/access scope remain implementation design to document
and test, not a mandate for a per-notice tombstone archive or a particular public token.

History-loss metadata must be access-safe and bounded. Conservative incomplete/uncertain reporting is permitted
where exact affected-history proof is unavailable; do not expose concealed scopes, notice counts, relationships or
deleted bodies through gap information. Define what happens when history-loss evidence itself is unavailable or
retired; it cannot silently turn missing history into complete evidence. TICKET-00046 owns public selection/access
binding, continuation and completeness responses, including changed permissions, using this qualified storage contract.

Coordinate deletion with producer/scope/identity comparison records. Explicitly document what minimal comparison
evidence is retained or removed, its lifetime, and the behavior of identical/conflicting retries at each stage.
Recheck recording permission on retries. Do not promise permanent duplicate suppression after the required evidence
is gone or preserve a hidden perpetual notice archive. Any accepted re-recording after guarantees end must follow
the documented contract rather than silently claim to be the original retained notice or original recovery position.
Exact repeat/missing-record and removed-identity semantics are implementation details within these boundaries.

### Transaction and publication boundary

A successful removal return follows actual commit of all related deletions/integrity/history-loss updates. Reuse
TASK-00158's explicit transaction ownership: roll back owned precommit failures; reject unsupported already-open
transactions before writes; do not commit or roll back unrelated caller work or acknowledge a savepoint as durable
completion. Response loss can leave the caller uncertain, so repeat behavior must remain safe and explicit.

Any defined removal fact event contains safe committed facts, not bodies, private diagnostics or receipt claims.
Use existing event contracts after actual commit. Later subscriber/publication failure leaves deletion committed
and is reported separately from storage failure. No automatic event replay/outbox or atomicity with unrelated
consumer operations is implied. Exact safe event catalog remains implementation design to document and qualify.

## Validation and permissions

Consumers supply current trusted removal decisions independent of read or recording permission. IDs, topic names,
recipient references, SUPER_ADMIN labels and retention cutoffs are not grants. Validate finite selections and
permitted scope/relationships before mutation; repeat and missing-record requests still require current admission.
Use safe unknown/denied behavior without leaking concealed records or raw database diagnostics.

The feed remains safe notices/references only. Common enforces supplied access and bounded structure, not universal
secret detection or consumer capture policy. Metadata/history-loss facts require protection too. Removal proves
neither subscriber receipt nor processing, expiry eligibility, business success or an authoritative Workflow transition.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Routed removal Commands atomically remove explicit bounded admitted records and related data on actual SQLite,
  MySQL and PostgreSQL while preserving unselected notices and their stable identities/positions.
- [ ] Removal uses independent current authority. Invalid/oversized/unauthorized selections cause no partial change;
  read permission, IDs, claimed cleanup identity and a cutoff cannot authorize mutation.
- [ ] History-loss updates commit atomically with deletion and survive reconstruction and an entirely empty feed;
  storage evidence permits honest incomplete/uncertain recovery without retaining deleted notice bodies indefinitely.
- [ ] Recovery metadata remains bounded/access-safe, including concealed scopes and retirement/unavailability of
  evidence. Absence/empty results alone never establish complete history, expiry or subscriber receipt.
- [ ] Repeat and missing-record behavior is documented/tested with fresh permission checks, including revocation
  between calls and no mutation of unrelated records.
- [ ] Concurrent deletion/recording/retry scenarios preserve survivors and ordering. Identical/conflicting retries
  follow the explicit comparison-record lifetime before/after removal without an indefinite deduplication claim.
- [ ] Injected failures across deletion/history-loss writes roll back all owned changes; unsupported enclosing
  transactions leave caller work untouched. Return occurs only after actual commit, not savepoint release.
- [ ] Safe fact events where defined occur after commit; later dispatch/publication failure cannot undo deletion
  or masquerade as storage rollback. No removed-body/private-diagnostic disclosure or delivery proof is introduced.
- [ ] TICKET-00046 receives qualified storage/recovery-limit contracts and fixtures for integrated public recovery
  without making either recording or removal depend on its future Query/Tool implementation.
- [ ] Schema/API compatibility, composition, limits, removal/retry/recovery behavior and consumer policy boundaries
  are documented with CONTEXT.md aligned; exact owned coverage and complete `./bin/build` pass with local evidence.
