# Publish retained safe notices through optional Mercure

## Outcome

Consumer composition can publish permitted retained safe notices through optional public/private Mercure output,
then inspect/recover retained data through the shared Queries even when publication fails. Deliver the complete
recorded notice → current publication policy → bounded Mercure update → shared recovery journey in one normal PR.
Mapping, permission enforcement, actual publisher integration and failure isolation are one vertical outcome.

John approved this single TASK as the complete decomposition of [TICKET-00048](../tickets/00048-TICKET.md).
Its only blocker is [TASK-00161](00161-TASK.md), transitively supplying durable recording/removal and shared notice
inspection/recovery. No dependency on MCP live TASK-00162, subscriber inspection, Prompts or introspection is added.
This completes EPIC-00011's TASK planning, not implementation or delivery.

## Scope

- In scope: explicit optional bounded publication operation and working post-commit composition, safe notice-to-update
  mapping, consumer-supplied current topic/disclosure policy and public/private publisher selection.
- In scope: real retained Command/Query/recovery integration on SQLite/MySQL/PostgreSQL, actual Common Mercure adapter
  translation with a controlled HubInterface, finite encoded output, safe observable failures and Mercure-free composition.
- In scope: public compatibility, documentation/CONTEXT.md, meaningful behavior tests, exact coverage and full local gate.
- Out of scope: dashboard/UI/navigation, hub deployment/operations, browser authentication/subscription implementation,

- Out of scope: another generic socket layer, new notice store/adapter SQL policy, automatic retry workers/outbox,
  delivery/processing ledgers, guaranteed hub replay, exactly-once publication or protected/full Task-state broadcasts.
- Out of scope: MCP JSON-RPC wire translation, new live subscriptions and automatic Resource/target reads.

and `EventDispatcher::trigger()`. Notice recording and application causation keep their existing owners. External
publication needs no new business Command or domain event solely to record an attempted send.

### Approved publication and persistence boundary

Reuse Application `Publisher::push()` and `PrivatePublisher::pushPrivate()` with existing
`MercureHubPublisher`/`PrivateMercureHubPublisher`. Keep Mercure classes in Adapter and preserve these public contracts;
no extra generic socket abstraction, automatic adapter activation or new principal model. Existing `symfony/mercure`
remains optional development/suggested composition, not a production requirement for the shared feed or MCP.

Supply an explicit bounded delivery operation and working post-commit composition with TASK-00158. Publication starts
only after actual committed notice recording, not a pre-commit event or savepoint release. Define the handoff from
committed evidence into the operation without accepting an untrusted assertion that arbitrary payloads were saved.
No database transaction spans hub I/O, and the integration must not commit/roll back unrelated caller work. A failure
before commit produces no publication; after commit a delivery failure neither erases the notice nor changes the
recording outcome to rollback. Keep the two outcomes distinguishable under the actual composed reporting contract.

No automatic retry worker, outbox or delivery ledger is introduced. Document repeated-attempt and deduplicated-recording
retry behavior: duplicate publication may occur, publisher success does not prove receipt/processing, and response loss
can leave delivery uncertain. Do not silently create an exactly-once promise or imply every recorded notice is eventually
published. Consumers own scheduling/retry choices; any later attempt applies current publication policy again.

Initial/history inspection and reconnect recovery use TASK-00160/00161's shared Queries, with no adapter SQL or competing
permission/completeness policy. Authorized polling remains available after hub failure, including truthful revocation,
removal and incomplete/uncertain-history outcomes. No hub replay, empty-page catch-up proof or recovery of unrecorded
application changes is inferred. Consumer reload/presentation/polling cadence remains outside Common.

### Approved mapping and policy boundary

Consumer-supplied current policy chooses permitted topic, public/private mode and disclosed fields. Read access to a
notice is not permission to broadcast it. Do not send to an audience broader than the permitted disclosure or derive
a public fallback from missing private support, denied policy or transport failure. Apply the decision before each
publication attempt. Document the policy context and permission-change limits: the publisher cannot independently
establish every browser subscriber's current entitlement or retract previously delivered bytes. Consumers own actual
hub/browser authentication and authorization. Topic vocabulary, recipients and SUPER_ADMIN labels are not grants.

Validate mapped topics, message shape, reference/field counts and finite encoded payload bounds before sending,
including escaping and multibyte content. Preserve stable notice identity and distinct subject Agent, intended recipients,
producer/author and causation references where disclosure permits. Preserve honest unknown/broadcast cases rather than
inventing identities or treating intended recipients as the confirmed audience. Topic/identity/causation metadata is
itself protected by supplied disclosure policy. Exact APIs, representation, numeric limits and any bounded multi-target
handling remain documented implementation details, including partial-failure semantics if multiple sends are supported.

Mercure updates are safe consumer-facing data, not MCP JSON-RPC subscription envelopes, complete wire transcripts,
protected messages/tool arguments/results/process output or full MCP Task state. Mapping must not automatically read
referenced Resources/files/network targets. Structural validation does not universally detect secrets; consumers remain
responsible for safe source metadata and redaction policy. Bounds must reject safely rather than silently truncate
meaningful fields or publish an incomplete notice as complete.

## Validation and permissions

Use trusted current consumer publication decisions, separate from notice-read/record access. Caller-provided IDs,
recipient fields, human permission, private flags and role labels cannot substitute for that decision. Validate
unsupported/unauthorized mode/topic/payload combinations before invoking either publisher; denied private publication
must not call the public publisher. Preserve current inspection/polling authorization rather than assuming permission
from a prior successful send or a retained reference.

Existing Mercure adapters preserve underlying exception text in `SocketException`; it is private diagnostic material,
not safe client output. The new integration reports denial, mapping/size failure and hub failure under a documented
safe observable contract without exposing credentials, protected bodies or raw exception details. Keep meaningful
redaction-aware diagnostics distinct from public failures. A diagnostic/reporting failure cannot undo committed data
or transform a failed send into a success claim. Exact reporting composition remains implementation design.
