# Correct Quoted Email Part Extraction

## Outcome

Consumers retrieve the correct local and domain parts of already accepted quoted email addresses without changing
email identity, canonicalization or accepted syntax. Deliver the repair, failing regression, compatibility/Doctrine
conversion evidence and documentation together in one independently reviewable PR under
[TICKET-00035](../tickets/00035-TICKET.md) and [EPIC-00008](../epics/00008-EPIC.md).

John approved this bug TASK and its contracts on 2026-10-06 as one of three independent contact/network slices.
There are no TASK dependencies or assigned execution priority. In particular, the repair does not require the new IP
or phone values in TASK-00132/TASK-00133. The EPIC's compatible next-minor scope applies, subject to actual compatibility
assessment; this is planning approval, not an exact-version decision or implementation/release authority.

## Scope

- In scope: EmailAddress::localPart()/domainPart() extraction for accepted quoted local parts containing at-signs,
  including escaped local-part bytes and quoted addresses with domain literals.
- In scope: preserve ordinary extraction, bracket stripping, original-string representation, case-sensitive
  equality/hash, lowercase canonical(), constructor/factory validation and supported exception behavior.
- In scope: direct regressions, helper and existing canonical/legacy Doctrine conversion evidence, public/behavioral
  compatibility assessment, docs/values.md and CONTEXT.md alignment.
- Out of scope: broader email validation/RFC support, quote decoding or removal, Unicode/IDNA additions, changed
  equality/login identity, new IP/phone adoption, reachability/DNS lookups, permission policy, persistence/schema or
  database-type changes, new dependencies, runtime deprecation warnings and packaging/release-process tests.

## Use cases and contracts

| Use case | Commands | Queries | Events | Expected side effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer retrieves parts of `"a@b"@example.com` | N/A; local value access | N/A; local parsing | N/A; no mutation | localPart returns `"a@b"` including quotes; domainPart returns `example.com`. |
| Consumer retrieves escaped quoted local bytes | N/A; local value access | N/A; local parsing | N/A; no mutation | Preserve the complete accepted local substring; do not unescape it. |
| Consumer retrieves a domain literal | N/A; local value access | N/A; local parsing | N/A; no mutation | Keep current bracket stripping and literal contents, including an accepted IPv6 prefix. |
| Consumer compares, canonicalizes or serializes an existing email | N/A; value operation | N/A; local comparison | N/A; no mutation | Original-string equality/hash/JSON and separately lowercased canonical() remain unchanged. |
| Consumer constructs through email() or reads/writes a Doctrine email value | N/A; existing helper/conversion | N/A; local conversion | N/A; no new event | Retain constructor/type conversion contracts and original stored address text. |

Construction/access performs no network I/O, delivery or new database operation. These values neither establish
identity authorization nor grant contact permission. Existing application policy and Doctrine persistence effects
remain with their current owners; this repair adds no new business commands, queries or events.

## Validation and permissions

Preserve existing Validate::isEmail-backed accepted syntax and DomainException rejection behavior, including public
factory/helper signatures. Find the actual delimiter separating the already validated local part from its domain,
not the first at-sign inside a quoted local part. Do not silently normalize component bytes, strip local quotes or
turn extraction into a new parser/validator contract. Keep domain-literal bracket handling and accepted literal
contents exactly as before, rather than delegating to the new IP hierarchy.

EmailAddress::toString(), string/JSON conversion and hash remain the original value; equals remains concrete-type
and case-sensitive. canonical() continues to lowercase the whole address as its separate existing operation; it
must not become the stored/equality form. Authentication identity, email ownership and permission to send remain
consumer-owned. Address syntax and diagnostic messages are not public-safe presentation promises.
